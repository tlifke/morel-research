PRINCIPLES
The following principles govern how the decisions above must be made.
[p01] (constraint; applies to: Revenue/Profit Sharing)
  Rule: A clause must show on its own that a revenue or profit share exists to be responsive; machinery that merely administers one — reporting obligations, report timing, objection and audit process, dispute resolution — is not Revenue/Profit Sharing.
  When to consider it: Consider when a candidate sentence is about a revenue share but describes process rather than entitlement, or when the sentence only makes sense given a percentage stated elsewhere.
[p02] (absence; applies to: Agreement Date)
  Rule: Claim absence when the document does not state the specific fact the target asks for, even where it states a neighbouring fact that plausibly stands in for it.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever the document supplies something adjacent to the requested value — an effective date where an execution date is asked for, a venue where a governing law is asked for — and the temptation is to answer with the near miss rather than claim absence.
[p03] (absence; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: A document that amends or restates an earlier agreement carries no responsive text for the clauses it reproduces from that agreement; only what the later document introduces is extracted.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever the document announces itself as amending, restating or supplementing an earlier agreement, and its operative clauses read as reproductions of that agreement's language.
[p04] (absence; applies to: Agreement Date)
  Rule: A partially redacted or blank agreement date is still an Agreement Date extraction, recorded with the unknown components bracketed rather than ruled absent; absence is claimed only when no agreement date appears in the introductory paragraph, on the cover page, or on the signature page.
  When to consider it: Consider when the date position exists but the value is missing — blanks, underscores, asterisks, an empty day slot, or a date phrase interrupted by a redaction marker.
[p05] (procedure; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: Every category in scope receives a ruling on every contract, and where no responsive text is found the category is recorded as having no answer rather than left blank.
  When to consider it: Consider on any contract where a category yields nothing — the ruling of absence is itself the annotation and is made explicitly, not by omission.
[p06] (disambiguation; applies to: Governing Law)
  Rule: Governing Law covers only a clause designating the substantive law that governs the contract as a whole; venue, forum, jurisdiction-consent and arbitration clauses are not responsive, and neither is a law that governs only one section of the contract.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a dispute-resolution or miscellaneous section is being read — these clauses frequently sit in the same paragraph as the true governing-law sentence, or replace it entirely.
[p07] (constraint; applies to: Agreement Date)
  Rule: Exactly one Agreement Date is extracted per contract and the span is the date text alone, excluding the surrounding execution wording and excluding any date appearing in a header, a footer, or a recital.
  When to consider it: Consider once the introductory paragraph has been read; the competing candidates are the recital dates, the running-header date, and the two signature-page dates.
[p08] (procedure; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: When the responsive material is not contiguous in the contract — a lead-in plus one of several subsections, a clause plus the definition it needs, or two clauses that must be read together — the span concatenates only the responsive fragments, in document order, separated by the marker "<omitted>", and drops the intervening non-responsive text.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever the responsive text sits inside an enumerated list, or depends on a defined term or on a date stated elsewhere in the contract.
[p09] (disambiguation; applies to: Volume Restriction)
  Rule: The volume-threshold target needs a consequence as well as a ceiling — a higher fee, or the counterparty's consent — and a bare quantity cap that states none is not responsive.
  When to consider it: Consider on any clause naming a cap, ceiling or maximum: check that the clause also says what happens once the cap is passed, and claim absence where it does not.
[p10] (preference; applies to: Agreement Date)
  Rule: Extract the minimal expression that answers the target question, clipped out of the sentence that carries it, rather than the whole containing sentence.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever the answer to a target is a short self-contained value — a date, a jurisdiction, a named amount — embedded in a longer recital or preamble sentence.
[p11] (disambiguation; applies to: Revenue/Profit Sharing)
  Rule: A payment obligation is Revenue/Profit Sharing only when the amount varies with the counterparty's revenue, profit, sales, margin or units sold; fixed amounts, milestone payments, fixed-amount tiered schedules, cost-based or reimbursement payments are not, however they are labelled in the contract.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a payment, royalty, commission or fee clause is a candidate — the word "royalty" is not evidence either way, the arithmetic is.
[p12] (constraint; applies to: Agreement Date, Governing Law, Expiration Date)
  Rule: The categories that ask for a single value — Agreement Date, Governing Law, Expiration Date — take exactly one span per contract, the one governing the agreement as a whole; a second such clause later in the document is a restatement or a section-specific variant and is not extracted.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a value has already been found and a second instance of the same kind appears — a choice-of-law sentence in an attached form, a term clause restated for one product line.
[p13] (disambiguation; applies to: Minimum Commitment, Volume Restriction)
  Rule: When two targets differ only in the direction of a threshold, assign a clause by which side of the threshold it binds on — a lower bound the obligated party must meet, or an upper bound above which supply is capped, conditioned or repriced.
  When to consider it: Consider on any clause naming a quantity threshold, especially where the same quantity, forecast or capacity is bounded in two places in one contract.
[p14] (constraint; applies to: Minimum Commitment)
  Rule: A guaranteed floor qualifies for the minimum-commitment target whichever party it binds, and whatever it is a floor on — supply, payment or access — not only a buyer's purchase obligation.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a clause states a guaranteed minimum quantity or amount but the obligated party is the supplier rather than the buyer, or the thing guaranteed is not a purchase; the target's one-line definition reads narrower than the convention.
[p15] (disambiguation; applies to: Minimum Commitment, Revenue/Profit Sharing)
  Rule: An obligation stated purely as a proportion of an outcome answers the sharing target, while the same obligation answers the minimum-commitment target once it also guarantees a fixed floor.
  When to consider it: Consider on any royalty, fee or revenue-share clause: check whether it names a guaranteed amount or a "minimum"/"at least" qualifier alongside the percentage.
[p16] (absence; applies to: Minimum Commitment)
  Rule: An obligation carrying no quantified threshold does not satisfy a target that asks for one, however emphatic the commitment language.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a clause reads as a strong undertaking — "commits to", "shall use commercially reasonable efforts", "agrees that" — but names no number, quantity or rate.
[p17] (preference; applies to: Governing Law)
  Rule: The span for the governing-law target ends at the named jurisdiction; the conflicts-of-law tail that follows is boilerplate found in documents with no such target at all, and is left outside the span.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever the choice-of-law sentence continues past the named jurisdiction into a "without regard to", "notwithstanding" or "excluding its" clause — the responsive material has already ended.
[p18] (constraint; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: A passage that independently satisfies more than one target's definition is extracted under each of them; targets are not mutually exclusive.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a single clause has already been assigned to one target and would also answer a second target's question on its own terms — resist the pull to spend the passage once.
[p19] (procedure; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: Document furniture interrupting a passage is carried along inside the extracted span, but furniture standing on its own is never extracted.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a page footer, redaction legend, filing-provenance line or page number falls in the middle of the passage being extracted, and the tidy instinct is to drop it or to split the passage in two.
[p20] (constraint; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: Annotation covers the executed agreement itself; responsive text that appears only in an exhibit, schedule, annex or appendix appended after the signature block is not extracted, even where that text would be responsive had it appeared in the body.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever the most on-point language for a category sits in a pricing schedule, a statement of work or a form of guaranty attached at the end of the document.
[p21] (disambiguation; applies to: Agreement Date)
  Rule: The Agreement Date is the date the parties executed the agreement, so where the introductory paragraph names one date and the signature pages another, the signature-page date is the one extracted; a preamble or cover-page date is taken only when the execution block carries none.
  When to consider it: Consider whenever a contract carries a date in more than one place — the common case is a preamble "as of" date recited months before the parties actually signed.
[p22] (constraint; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: Each extracted span is one complete sentence, period to period, and where the responsive text is a numbered subsection it includes the lead-in clause needed to make that subsection read as a complete sentence.
  When to consider it: Whenever choosing span boundaries for any category whose decision is yes/no-shaped (all three of the confusable trio); does not govern the date and party categories, which the Handbook exempts.
[p23] (procedure; applies to: all targets)
  Rule: A sentence responsive to more than one category is extracted under every category it is responsive to; assigning it to one category does not remove it from the others.
  When to consider it: Consider when a single passage does two jobs at once — e.g. a purchase obligation that also fixes a percentage payment, or a payment schedule that also sets a volume threshold.
