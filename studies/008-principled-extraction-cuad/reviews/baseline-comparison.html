Study 008 beside the CUAD paper: which comparisons are legitimate?

Three sets of numbers now exist. Two of them can be put on the same axes; only one of those two means what a reader will assume it means; the comparison that would settle the question has not been run and cannot be run before gate G4.

legitimateTheir published performance, reproduced exactly

CUAD's three released checkpoints score AUPR 42.586 / 48.250 / 47.793 against published 42.6 / 48.2 / 47.8 on the official test split (4,182 questions, 41 categories). This is the honest reference for what a task-specific fine-tuned extractor achieves. It says nothing about us.

ceiling onlySame scorer, but a memorised opponent — their models vs ours on harness_val

Same scorer, same contracts, same 12 categories — and their models were fine-tuned on these contracts. Their AUPR here is 0.688–0.744 against their honest 0.426–0.482, with the published model ordering scrambled. Usable as a memorised ceiling and as a diagnostic on annotation convention. Not usable to say who is better.

Different contracts, different category set (41 vs 12), different gold pool. There is no arithmetic that makes AUPR 48.2 on test comparable to precision 0.751 on harness_val. The clean comparison needs our committed decision on test, which is sealed until gate G4.

2. Their honest reference — the curve, and their Figure 4

The precision–recall curves on the official test split, plotted from the checked-in 102-point sweep using the interpolated-precision column their get_aupr integrates and their P@R figures read off. Hollow markers are the exact operating points at 80% recall.

Their Figure 4, recomputed — and why we cannot appear in it

The CUAD paper's Figure 4 is captioned “Comparison of AUPR for DeBERTa-xlarge across different label categories. While performance is high for some labels, it is has much room for improvement for other labels.”: per-category AUPR, a single model (DeBERTa-xlarge), horizontal bars, sorted descending. Figure 2 below matches that presentation, recomputed from the authors' shipped predictions with exact-match category subsetting rather than evaluate.py's substring filter — the substring filter leaks on Insurance (102 exact questions against 142 substring matches), and exact match is the correct subsetting.

No bar of ours belongs on this axis — not because an AUPR is impossible for us, but because this run has no ranking to sweep. AUPR summarises a threshold sweep over scored candidates; their extractive head emits a top-20 candidate list per question with probabilities, swept by one global threshold. Our output contract emits a single committed decision with no score attached, which is a design choice in the contract rather than a property of prompting — D-32 was corrected on this point on 2026-08-17, and plans/comparability-plan.md records what producing a ranking would take (sampling frequency, sequence logprob, or teacher-forced candidate likelihood via Tinker's TopKPromptLogprobs) and why the resulting AUPR would be a fair system-level comparison rather than a like-for-like measurement of the same construct. Until that exists, Figure 5 below is the closest comparable per-category view we can honestly draw — micro-F1 at the operating point, on harness_val — and it is a different quantity on a different split, which is why it is a separate figure rather than extra bars here.

Two things fall out of the overlay, and both belong in the interpretation.

Our 12 categories are the easy end of their 41. Mean AUPR 0.608 across our subset against 0.393 across the other 29; nine of our twelve sit in the top half of their ranking, including their three best-performing clause types. Any absolute number we report on this subset is flattered relative to a full-corpus result, and a future test-split comparison must either recompute their side on the same 12 or be read as category-mismatched.

Our recomputed ordering is not their published ordering. Rank agreement is Spearman ρ = 0.861 over the 40 categories their figure labels — strong at the top (their top five and ours share four members) and loose in the middle: we place Source Code Escrow at #19 against their #11, and Volume Restriction at #36 against their #24. The pooled Table 2 figures reproduce to four decimal places, so the scoring path is not in doubt; the per-category divergence is unexplained and is a caveat on Figure 2, not on §1.

Their figure plots 40 categories, not 41. The caption and body say all 41; the axis carries 40 labels. The one absent is Price Restrictions, which scores AUPR 0.000 for all three released checkpoints in our reproduction. We plot it, at zero.

3. The harness_val head-to-head — and why the banner matters more than the figure

These are memorised scores. Read the figure as a ceiling, not as an opponent. The 40 harness_val contracts sit inside CUAD's official train split, i.e. inside these three checkpoints' fine-tuning data. Their AUPR here is 0.688 / 0.744 / 0.718 against the reproduced Table 2 values of 0.426 / 0.482 / 0.478, and the model ordering is scrambled relative to the published one. Nothing in this section is evidence that our system is better or worse than theirs.

Second caveat, equally load-bearing. At any single confidence threshold their models emit at most one span per question. On categories whose gold carries several spans per contract — License Grant averages 3.81 gold spans per gold-present question here, Source Code Escrow 7.00 — their recall is structurally capped near 1 / spans-per-question. Their apparent weakness there is an artifact of the comparison. That is why every per-category number below is reported at two thresholds.

Our C2 sits at precision 0.719 / recall 0.430 and C3 at 0.751 / 0.430 — between their conf 0.5 and conf 0.7 points. A 9B general model with a prompt lands at the same operating point as a fine-tuned extractor that has been shown the key for these documents. That is interesting; it is not a capability claim, because the two sides reach that point through completely different errors (§5).

The two sides' question pools are not quite identical, and the reviews do not say so. Their run covers all 40 harness_val contracts (480 questions, 211 gold-present, 336 gold spans). Ours covers the 38-contract C2∩C3 intersection over 3 seeds, and per-trial dropout (parse failures, API errors) removes further question sets: C2 scores 103 trials / 1,236 questions, C3 100 / 1,200. The resulting gold-present share differs — 44.0% for them against 41.2% (C2) and 41.7% (C3) for us. Small, but it means §3–§5 are same-scorer and same-category rather than strictly same-question.

Finding 2 — Governing Law precision: 0.97–1.00 theirs, 0.42 ours

Their precision is 0.97–1.00; ours is 0.42 (C2) and 0.44 (C3), on 44 and 42 false positives. Every one of those false positives falls on a gold-present question (0 on gold-absent questions), so we are not failing to find the clause — we are adding venue, forum and arbitration clauses beside it. Their models were trained on annotations that exclude those, and they exclude them. This is independent corroboration of the same convention that principle w03 exists to state, and w03 is the one record in the working set with checker_status: usable. It is not yet fixing this: C2 and C3 carry essentially the same false positives.

Finding 3 — Volume Restriction is poor for everyone

They reach recall 0.33–0.50 only by holding precision at 0.12–0.14; at conf 0.1 RoBERTa-large is 0.12 / 0.67. We score 0.14 / 0.17 and 0.00 / 0.00. A model that memorised this corpus cannot make Volume Restriction precise either, which is the strongest available evidence that the category's boundary is genuinely disputed in the gold rather than merely hard for us. It is a reason not to write a principle for it off this split.

Their false positives are 89–95% over-claims on gold-absent questions, with near-perfect span boundaries. Ours are 68–72% wrong boundaries on gold-present questions. The decline rates are nearly identical — 22–26% theirs against 26–28% ours — so both systems lose about the same recall to silence and then differ completely in what they do when they speak. We over-claim on 4–5% of gold-absent questions against their 17–22%.

Read as system character: conservative-and-sloppy-bounded against aggressive-and-precisely-bounded. That tracks the task framings exactly. Ours is prompted for a committed decision that has to be defensible, including a decision to say nothing; theirs is a recall-first shortlist for a human reviewer, swept over a threshold. It also means span fidelity and absence discipline are separable problems for us — any principle that improves boundaries is not in tension with the absence machinery, which is the half already working.

6. Our own contrast, C2 vs C3, is a null

On the registered headline metric — CUAD-scorer micro-F1 — the difference is +0.0230 [-0.0102, +0.0578]. Recall is +0.0152 [-0.0156, +0.0480]. Precision is +0.0400 with an interval whose lower bound sits on zero.

The precision interval is not stable across RNG seeds, so it is not a conclusion. 7. The comparison that would settle it, and what it needs

The clean comparison is: our committed decision and their released checkpoints, both on the official test split, both scored by evaluate.py, on the same category set. It does not exist yet. Concretely it requires:

  • Gate G4 — a deliberate human go/no-go opening the 102-contract test split, which no part of this study has loaded. Everything on this page is pre-G4 development evidence.
  • Our run on test at whatever condition the ladder settles on. As the output contract stands that produces one committed-decision point rather than a curve; adding a candidate score would produce a curve, at the cost of the comparison becoming system-level rather than like-for-like.
  • A decision on the category axis. Their published figures pool 41 categories; we run 12. A 12-category recomputation of their side is a different quantity from Table 2 and must never be printed beside it. Either we recompute theirs at 12 from their shipped predictions, or the comparison is category-mismatched and inadmissible — and Figure 2 shows the mismatch is not neutral: our 12 average AUPR 0.608 for their best model against 0.393 for the categories we left out.
  • An aggregate that both sides can occupy. Their headline is AUPR; we do not have one yet — it needs a scored ranking our output contract does not currently emit, and plans/comparability-plan.md costs out the three ways to get one. Micro-F1 at the operating point is the aggregate both sides can stand on, and it is what §4's Figure 5 uses — but a single committed point compared against a model free to pick a threshold favours whoever chose better, and the pre-registration should fix which of their operating points is the reference before the split opens.
  • An honest treatment of the single-point-versus-curve mismatch. As long as we report a point and they report a curve, where our point lands relative to their operating points is the only defensible reading, and the pre-registration on that should stand before the split opens.

Until then, the memorised-ceiling comparison is what we have, and it answers a different question: not “are we good” but “what does competence on this annotation convention look like, and which of our gaps are convention gaps.”

8. Reading

The honest summary of this page is that it contains one confirmed reproduction, one diagnostic, and one null — and no result about whether our approach works. The reproduction is solid and does exactly one job: it certifies the scoring path, so that when a real comparison becomes available the scorer will not be the thing in doubt. The diagnostic is genuinely informative but only in one direction. Where their memorised models are near-perfect and we are not — duration-expressed Expiration Dates at 1.00 against 0.00, Governing Law precision 1.00 against 0.42 — we learn that the CUAD annotation convention is learnable and that we have not learned it, which is actionable. Where nobody does well, as in Volume Restriction, we learn something about the gold rather than about any model. But where we appear to beat them, at conf 0.5 on the multi-span categories, the advantage mostly evaporates at conf 0.1, and even the residue — License Grant, where they do not overtake us at any threshold — is a comparison against a model that was shown these answers in training. It cannot be cashed as a win.

Figure 2 adds a caution that applies to every absolute number on this page, including ones that will survive to test. The twelve categories this study works on are the easy end of CUAD: their best model averages AUPR 0.608 across our subset and 0.393 across the 29 we excluded, with nine of our twelve in the top half of their ranking. The subset was chosen for other reasons — annotation tractability and principle coverage — but the consequence is the same: a precision of 0.751 here is not a precision of 0.751 on CUAD, and any headline we eventually publish has to say which twelve.

The C2/C3 contrast is a null, and it should be reported as one. Micro-F1 moves +0.0230 with an interval from -0.0102 to +0.0578; the precision effect that looked promising flips sign-of-verdict across RNG seeds. Two things bound how much that null can be made to mean. First, 38 contracts is the binding constraint, not the effect size — a better-powered run could still separate the arms, and Exclusivity is where to look. Second, and more limiting: the principle set is unselected. Nine of its ten records carry checker_status: needs_rebuild, so what C3 actually tests is the citation requirement layered on an unvetted set, not the principles. A null here is evidence about requiring a citation. It is close to no evidence about whether good principles help.

Which points at what this study's result will actually be. It is not any comparison to the CUAD paper's models — those are a fixed reference, one honest and one memorised, and neither is the thing under test. The result is the iteration-0-to-ladder delta: whether principled iteration moves our own numbers on our own split, measured against our own starting point, with the CUAD scorer holding the measurement fixed. The three findings in §4 are useful precisely because they are hypotheses for that ladder — the Expiration Date convention, the Governing Law exclusion, the span-width clipping — each with a memorised model's behaviour as an existence proof that the target is reachable. That is what this page licenses. Nothing on it licenses a claim that we beat CUAD's models, or that they beat us.
