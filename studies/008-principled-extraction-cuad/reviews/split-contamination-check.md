# Split contamination check — near-duplicate contracts across CUAD splits

**VERDICT: the splits are NOT safe as built. Two holdout contracts have
near-duplicate twins in ft_train, one of them a verbatim substring — so the
Phase 2 fine-tuning pool currently contains the sealed evaluation set.**

**Minimal fix: drop 4 contracts from ft_train (368 → 364). Holdout and dev are
untouched, no re-split, no reseeding. Proposal only — not executed; splits stay
frozen pending G1 and this is Tyler's call.**

Generated by `scripts/scan_split_contamination.py`; machine-readable evidence in
`reviews/split-contamination-check.json`. Read-only over holdout: this check
compares text and `is_impossible` flags for contamination purposes only, and no
holdout content is reproduced here or in the JSON.

## Method

Exhaustive, all 510 contracts, all 129,795 pairs. No sampling, no blocking
heuristic that could miss a pair.

- **Exact.** SHA-256 over NFKC-normalized, whitespace-collapsed text. Raw
  byte-identity is hashed and reported separately.
- **Near.** 5-gram word shingles over the token stream from
  `mine_contrastive_pairs.normalize` (NFKC → lowercase → digits `<num>` →
  redaction runs `<redacted>` → whitespace collapse), reusing that module rather
  than introducing a third metric. All-pairs shingle intersections come from a
  sparse `doc × shingle` incidence matrix multiplied by its transpose, which is
  exact rather than a MinHash estimate — 510 docs and 2.9M distinct shingles make
  this cheap enough (~5s) that approximation buys nothing.
- **Metrics.** `jaccard = |A∩B| / |A∪B|` and
  **`containment = |A∩B| / min(|A|,|B|)`**. Containment is the one that matters
  here: when a short contract is re-filed inside a longer compound document,
  Jaccard collapses toward the length ratio and hides the leak, while containment
  goes to 1.0. The worst case found below has containment 1.000 and Jaccard 0.214.
- **Flag threshold.** `jaccard ≥ 0.60 OR containment ≥ 0.80`. Listing floor is
  lower (`jaccard ≥ 0.30 OR containment ≥ 0.80`) so the near-miss band is visible
  and the threshold can be judged rather than trusted.
- Flagged pairs additionally get a literal substring test on whitespace-normalized
  text, which is what upgrades "similar" to "verbatim copy".

**Threshold calibration.** The gap between flagged and unflagged is wide and
clean. The highest unflagged cross-split pair is Neoforma vs Paperexchange
co-branding (containment 0.529, Jaccard 0.351) — two genuinely different
contracts sharing boilerplate, not the same document. Nothing sits between
0.53 and 0.83 on either metric across the whole corpus.

**Pair 1 is the serious one.** The **ft_train** entry is the shorter document —
7,042 chars against the holdout contract's 32,371 — and it appears **verbatim
inside** the holdout filing. It is the IP-rights / confidentiality **annex**
extracted as its own CUAD entry: it ends at exhibit marker `A-2` and carries no
signing date, which is also why its gold has Agreement Date absent. The holdout
entry is the complete filing, annex included.

The contamination is the same either way — 100% of that ft_train document's text
sits inside a holdout contract, so fine-tuning on it trains directly on holdout
material. What the direction changes is the *share* of the holdout instance
exposed: roughly 22% of it (7,042 of 32,371 chars), not all of it. The low
Jaccard (0.214) is still exactly why a Jaccard-only scan would have missed this:
the two documents differ 4.6× in length, and only containment surfaces it. The
9-of-12 gold disagreement follows from the two entries annotating different
documents — an annex vs the whole filing — so the pair leaks *text* while
teaching *different labels*.

**Pair 2** is the same content licence filed twice, once in a 2015 8-K and once
in a 2016 10-K. Not byte-identical (the two filings differ in boilerplate and
some redactions) but 94.6% containment, and the gold labels agree on all 12
subset categories — i.e. training on the ft_train copy teaches the holdout copy's
answers directly.

One further ft_train × holdout pair sits below threshold and is **not**
contamination: `NeoformaInc…Co-Branding` vs `PaperexchangeComInc…Co-Branding`,
containment 0.529 / Jaccard 0.351, different parties, gold disagrees on
Agreement Date and Minimum Commitment. Genre similarity, not a re-filing.

## 5. Correction to the reported evidence

The duplicate-detection work described the SINA/Leju content licence as
"byte-identical text, filed twice, opposite Agreement Date labels". Measured
here, that is not byte-identical:
`ChinaRealEstateInformationCorp…Content License Agreement` vs
`LejuHoldingsLtd…Content License Agreement1` is 0.989 containment / 0.945
Jaccard and its gold **agrees** on all 12 subset categories; the Agreement Date
disagreement is with the *other* Leju entry, `…Content License Agreement2`, which
is a 2,180-char fragment (0.945 containment, 0.043 Jaccard). Both Leju entries
are in ft_train, so neither crosses a split boundary.

Across all 510 contracts there is exactly **one** byte-identical group and one
whitespace-normalization-identical group, and they are the same group: the ADURO
pair. Every other duplicate in this report is near, not exact. This does not
change any conclusion — it changes which case is the exact one.

## 6. Recommendation

Holdout is the official CUAD test split and must not change; comparability to
the CUAD literature depends on it staying at exactly those 102 contracts. Dev
was just re-stratified under D-13 and any removal would perturb the
length-profile match. **So the entire fix belongs on the ft_train side, which is
a pool with 368 contracts and no size commitment.**

**Proposed minimal fix — drop 4 contracts from ft_train, 368 → 364:**

| drop from ft_train | reason |
|---|---|
| `WOMENSGOLFUNLIMITEDINC_03_29_2000-EX-10.13-ENDORSEMENT AGREEMENT - Intellectual Property Rights Confidentiality and Non-Use Obligations Agreement` | contained verbatim within a holdout contract (this entry is the 7,042-char annex; the holdout filing is 32,371 chars) |
| `IdeanomicsInc_20160330_10-K_EX-10.26_9512211_EX-10.26_Content License Agreement` | 0.946 containment with a holdout contract, gold agrees |
| `ADUROBIOTECH,INC_06_02_2020-EX-10.7-CONSULTING AGREEMENT` | byte-identical to a dev contract |
| `ARMSTRONGFLOORING,INC_01_07_2019-EX-10.2-INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY AGREEMENT` | 0.975 containment with a dev contract |

Properties of this fix: holdout stays at 102, dev stays at 40 with its D-13
length profile intact, the seed and the sampling procedure are unchanged, and no
contract moves between splits — the only change is four deletions from the pool
that is never evaluated on. It is also strictly reversible.

**Two decisions that are separable from the fix and should not be bundled into
it:**

1. **ft_train-internal duplicates (§4).** Five clusters double-weight the same
   contract during fine-tuning. That is a Phase 2 training-hygiene question, not
   a split-safety one, and the right place to settle it is when the Phase 2 data
   loader is written. No action needed at G1.
2. **Twins with disagreeing gold.** ADURO's two entries are byte-identical yet
   labeled differently on Anti-Assignment and Exclusivity; the WOMENSGOLF and
   NETGEAR pairs likewise. This is direct evidence of CUAD annotation noise on
   exactly the categories the study scores, and it puts a ceiling on achievable
   agreement that no model or prompt can cross. It belongs in inv 003's
   reliability discussion and should be quoted in the study's limitations.

**Standing guard, recommended regardless of the above.** Add a post-split
assertion to `build_dataset.py` that recomputes normalized content hashes and
cross-split containment and fails the build if any cross-split pair exceeds the
threshold. Disjointness is currently asserted on `contract_id`, which is the CUAD
title string — as this check demonstrates, identical content under two different
titles defeats that assertion completely. Without the guard, any future re-split
can silently reintroduce the same problem. I have not added it, because it would
fail the build as things currently stand and the fix above is Tyler's call.

Nothing in this report has been executed. Splits remain exactly as built.
