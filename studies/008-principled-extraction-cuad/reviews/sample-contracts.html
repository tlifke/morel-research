Read this first

Split. All three contracts were drawn from the pre-carve training pool (368 contracts, the split then called ft_train), never from harness_val (40) or test (102). Under the post-INV1-D8 arrangement the short contract is in model_train and the mid and long contracts are in principle_train; the page was built before that carve and its counts are over the 368-contract pool. harness_val and test have to stay unexamined: harness_val is where P0 and the condition grid get measured and test is the final report set, so any human or model exposure to them contaminates the numbers this study exists to produce. model_train is reserved for Phase 2 fine-tuning, where looking at examples is exactly the point. Nothing on this page should be read as evidence about the model's test behaviour; it is evidence about the data.

Gold is multi-span. A CUAD category is not a slot with one answer. Most present categories carry several disjoint spans scattered across the document (here: up to 10, for Minimum Commitment in the long contract). The annotators marked every passage that evidences the category, so a model that finds the one "best" clause is partially right by construction, and span-level scoring has to decide what to do with the other ten. Absence is the other half: a category with is_impossible = true has zero spans and the correct output is a positive claim that the contract does not contain it.

Quirks observed while reading these three contracts

Everything below was seen directly in these three instances, not assumed. Most of it will make span-level scoring awkward.

  1. Gold spans overlap across categories, sometimes byte-identically. In contract 1, License Grant [4341:4672] and Exclusivity [4341:4672] are the same characters under two labels; in contract 2 the same is true of License Grant / Exclusivity [1613:2052]. A per-category span-F1 handles this fine, but any "assign each character one label" evaluation or any confusion-matrix built from character overlap will be wrong. It also means a model that emits one span for both categories is fully correct twice.
  2. Near-identical spans with off-by-one ends. Contract 1 has License Grant [6689:6965] and Exclusivity [6689:6964] — the same passage, one character apart. That is annotation jitter, not a distinction, and it sets a floor on how precisely any span-boundary metric can be interpreted.
  3. Spans nest. Contract 1's Minimum Commitment [5349:6056] strictly contains both Revenue/Profit Sharing [5349:5616] and [5883:6056]. Token-level Jaccard copes; "did the model find the span" as a set-membership question does not.
  4. Redacted text is inside the contract. Contracts 1 and 3 are confidential-treatment filings: every commercial number is literally ***** (29 and 45 occurrences respectively). Several gold spans are mostly asterisks — "an additional minimum recoupable guarantee of ***** dollars (US$*****)". So the Minimum Commitment and Revenue/Profit Sharing decisions have to be made on clause structure alone, with the amounts removed. Good news for the study (it forces rule-following over number-spotting), bad news for anyone assuming the text carries the facts.
  5. SEC page furniture is embedded mid-sentence, and gold splits around it. Contract 3 contains \n\n27\n\nSource: PLAYBOY ENTERPRISES INC, 10-Q/A, 2/20/2009\n\n\n\n\n\n in the middle of a sentence. The annotators stopped the Anti-Assignment span at [95903:96755] ending "…the ability and intention, to" and started a new one at [96820:…] beginning lowercase "adequately invest…". One legal thought, two gold spans, split by a page footer. A model that emits the whole passage as one span gets penalised for the footer characters; one that emits two gets rewarded for reproducing a scanning artifact.
  6. At least one span looks plainly mislabeled. Contract 3's License Grant [2764:2773] is the nine characters "Business." — the tail of "…marketing and promotion of the Playboy Commerce Business." That is not a license grant; it is a truncated selection. It will read as a false negative for any correct model.
  7. Other spans look labeled by neighbourhood rather than content. Contract 2's Minimum Commitment [5474:5516] is "Either Party may terminate this Agreement:" — a termination heading, captured presumably because the sub-clause below it is about failing to make an AMC payment. Expect a systematic class of gold spans that are structurally adjacent to the concept rather than expressing it.
  8. Boilerplate repeats verbatim, and gold marks each copy. Contract 1 has two Revenue/Profit Sharing spans at [2121:2448] and [3500:3824] whose text is character-identical for the first ~320 characters (the same royalty paragraph applied to a different property). A model emitting the passage once cannot be distinguished from one that missed the second occurrence unless scoring is offset-aware — and the TaskOutput schema carries text, not offsets, so it cannot be.
  9. Whitespace is not paragraph structure. These texts use runs of two or more spaces where a line break belongs: contract 2 has 692 double-space runs against only 208 newlines across 75k characters; contract 3 has 1,119. Words are also broken by the original line-wrap and survive as "non- transferable", "non-assi…". Any whitespace normalisation before scoring changes character offsets; any tokenizer will see these as split words.
  10. OCR noise, low but present. Contract 2 contains ÿ used as a bullet ("Terms and Conditions ÿ Price proposals are valid…") and ¼ in a numeric context ("quarterly targets at ¼ of annual amount"), plus ®/™ glyphs. Contract 3 uses · and • as bullets. No CRs, no tabs in any of the three.
  11. Date spans are tiny; clause spans are not. Agreement Date is 16–17 characters in all three contracts; clause categories run 100–1,100. A macro average over categories therefore mixes a near-exact-match task with a fuzzy-boundary task, and one wrong character hurts the date far more than the clause. Length stratification is already in the metrics plan; per-category stratification by span length may be needed too.
  12. One contract is a fragment of a filing. Contract 1 is …Content License Agreement2: CUAD split a single SEC exhibit into four separate contract records (1–4), and record 4 has just two present categories in 475 tokens. Absences in a fragment are not the same event as absences in a whole agreement — the clause may exist, just in sibling record 1. Worth knowing before absence accuracy is reported as if all absences were equivalent.
  13. Present categories are usually the same ones. Across the three, the head categories (Agreement Date, Governing Law, Expiration Date, License Grant) are present nearly always and the rare ones nearly never. Absence accuracy will be dominated by the tail, and a model that answers "absent" for Source Code Escrow and Most Favored Nation unconditionally will score well on absence without doing anything.
